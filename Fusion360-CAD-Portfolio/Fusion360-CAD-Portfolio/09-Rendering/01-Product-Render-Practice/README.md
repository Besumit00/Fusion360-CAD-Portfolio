# Product Render Practice

## Objective
Take a previously modeled part and practice Fusion 360's Render workspace — materials, appearances, scene lighting, and camera framing — to produce presentation-quality images for this portfolio.

## Skills Practiced
- Appearances vs Materials
- Scene/environment lighting (studio, outdoor)
- Camera settings (depth of field, focal length)
- In-cloud vs local rendering

## CAD Features Used
- No new modeling features — this is a rendering-only exercise on an existing model

## Challenges
- Renders looking flat/plasticky under default lighting.
- Long render times for high-quality output on a laptop GPU.

## How I Solved Them
Switched from the default environment to a studio HDRI environment with three-point lighting, and used Fusion's cloud rendering credits for the final high-resolution pass instead of rendering locally.

## Engineering Notes
Good rendering is about lighting and camera angle far more than raw settings — a well-lit 45-degree isometric shot communicates a product design better than a technically 'perfect' render with flat lighting.
